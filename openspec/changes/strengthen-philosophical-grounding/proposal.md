## Why

The paper is called *Forgetting* and argues for externalising memory, but it
draws almost nothing from the philosophy of memory or of knowledge. Its ground
is Heidegger, Nietzsche, Frankfurt and Bratman, which is about action and
attention. Five gaps follow from that.

- **It defends one kind of forgetting and practises two.** The paper argues
  for forgetting into a store, where the thing is kept and not recalled.
  particulars 0.3 and the episodes draft also erase, by redaction, and the
  episodes README says that a source being deleted is not a request to forget.
  Nothing in the paper justifies erasure, or says why it must be explicit.
- **It meets no objection.** The strongest case against the provenance spine,
  that a rational limited mind shouldn’t keep track of its reasons, is
  Harman’s, and the oldest case against externalised memory is Plato’s. The
  paper cites neither.
- **Its central convention has a literature it doesn’t use.** Recording who
  wrote what is an epistemology of testimony and of memory as preservation,
  and the line between what a person stated and what an assistant inferred is
  the line between avowal and attribution.
- **DKF is named after dialectic and the paper never cites Hegel.**
- **§11 asks for an argument it could already give.** Settled plans and bad
  faith has an answer in Ricoeur’s distinction between sameness and selfhood.

## What Changes

- §1 gets a new subsection, *Two kinds of forgetting* (`two-forgettings`),
  after *Active forgetting*: Ricoeur’s forgetting by effacement and reserve
  forgetting, Borges’ Funes and Mayer-Schönberger on total recall, and why
  erasure is redaction and only ever the person’s act.
- §1 *Becoming who you are*: one paragraph on narrative identity (MacIntyre,
  Schechtman) and Strawson’s objection, with a draft note.
- §1 *What this asks of the store*: Plato’s *Phaedrus* as the oldest objection
  and what answers it; Sutton’s complementarity and Adams and Aizawa’s
  objection to the extended mind.
- §4 gets a new subsection, *Why keep reasons at all* (`keeping-reasons`):
  Harman’s coherence theory and clutter avoidance, the debriefing results, and
  why an external store takes the foundations side for Harman’s own reasons.
- §4: a paragraph on testimony and preservative memory (Hardwig, Coady,
  Burge), and one on avowal and attribution (Moran).
- §5: a paragraph on Hegel’s *Aufhebung*, and an honest note that the
  thesis–antithesis vocabulary is not Hegel’s.
- §6 gets a new subsection, *Settled plans and bad faith* (`bad-faith`):
  Sartre, Ricoeur’s *idem* and *ipse*, Korsgaard. §11’s open question is cut
  down to what remains open.
- §8 *Episodic memory*: a paragraph on reconstructive memory (Bartlett,
  Schacter, Michaelian) and why episodes is deliberately unlike it.
- References gain every new work. The contents list is unchanged, since only
  subsections are added. `docs/README.md` lists the three new ids.
